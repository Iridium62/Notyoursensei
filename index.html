Path DCome on a working holiday visa

Japan has working holiday agreements with 30-plus countries — a year in-country with the right to work, no employer or sponsor required. It's not for everyone: age caps sit around 18–30 (18–25 for a handful of countries unless you extend), it's usually a one-shot deal per lifetime, and it isn't open to every passport — Americans, notably, don't get this option at all. But if yours qualifies, it's one of the fastest, lowest-friction ways to get here and start building something.

  • Whether your passport actually qualifies, and the real age cutoff for your country
  • How to use the year to build toward something instead of spending it as one long trip
  • Lining up work, or at least real leads, before you land, so the clock isn't ticking from zero
  • Turning the working holiday into an actual work visa before the year runs out
  • Being upfront about the ceiling — it's temporary, it isn't a work visa, and the clock doesn't stop
Path EStudy abroad on a language visa

A student visa gets you here long-term while you build the Japanese you actually need — the kind that comes from daily life, not another textbook. Some people finance it and go full-time; others save up first and go part-time. Either way, it buys you real time in the country and immersion no app or class back home gets you close to.

  • Comparing schools that build your Japanese, not just stack up certificates
  • Realistic costs, and how people actually fund this — savings, part-time work, or a mix
  • Typical visa conditions — expect a cap around 28 hours a week of part-time work, and it varies by school and stage
  • Using the visa as a soft landing — starting the job search while you're already here
  • What it doesn't do: it's not a work visa, and you need a next step lined up before it runs out
Path GStudy a degree, or intern, here

This goes further than Path E's language-school route — a full degree (a bachelor's exchange year, a Master's, or a PhD) and internships that plug into any of it. I went through study abroad myself, and I've since seen people combine these in all sorts of ways — study leading into an internship, an internship turning into a full-time offer, straight into a Master's, or all three back to back. I've also built up contacts on the school and internship side over the years, so where it makes sense, I can put you directly in front of them.

  • If your degree already includes an exchange or "sandwich" year, using it well beats treating it as a formality — how to actually make it count
  • Exchange years, direct-enrollment degrees, and MEXT-scholarship or self-funded routes — compared honestly on cost and how competitive each one is
  • Research-based Master's or PhD programs: whether you need a professor to sponsor you before you even apply, and how that conversation actually goes
  • Internships that plug into or replace a study stint — paid and unpaid, and what's realistic to land
  • An introduction through my own contacts on the school or internship side, when it's a genuine fit
  • Turning any of this into a long-term stay — the visa and job-hunt options once your program or internship ends
Path FTransfer with your current company

If your company already has an office here, or is looking to expand into Japan, an internal transfer is the safest path there is — you keep your employer and your track record instead of starting a cold job search from scratch.

  • How to actually check whether your company has a Japan presence — most people never ask
  • How to raise it internally without sounding like you're already halfway out the door
  • What a typical relocation package includes, so you know what's reasonable to ask for
  • What to expect if it moves forward — relocation support, timeline, visa handled through the company
  • An honest read on whether this door is even open for you — it isn't for everyone
Path HTry it on a digital nomad visa

Japan's digital nomad visa lets you live here for up to six months while working remotely for a foreign employer or your own overseas clients — no Japan-based sponsor, no quitting your job first. It's a narrow door: roughly ¥10 million a year (about $68,000 USD) in non-Japanese income, a passport from one of the roughly 50 eligible countries, and private health insurance for the whole stay, since this visa doesn't get you onto Japan's national health insurance. It's also one-shot — non-renewable, and once it's up you're outside Japan for about six months before you can apply again.

  • Whether your income, passport, and employer situation actually clear the bar — worth checking before building any plan around this
  • Using the six months as a real trial run — a neighborhood, a routine, whether daily life here holds up — instead of a long vacation
  • What it doesn't do: it isn't a path to residency by itself and won't convert into a work visa on its own — that still means one of the other paths
  • Lining the six months up so it sets up whichever path (teaching, IT, a transfer) you'd actually want next, instead of resetting to zero when you leave
  • Spouses and children can come too, but each has to clear the income and insurance bar on their own — it isn't a dependent visa

Best fit if you already clear that income bar remotely and nothing else on this page quite fits yet. Not a substitute for immigration advice on your specific situation.

before you book

Questions people actually ask

Skipping the FAQ fluff — these are the real doubts people email me with before booking.

How much Japanese will I need?

More than none — no sugar coating. Some people have the finances to move out and go straight into full-time language school. Most don't: they study alongside their current job and come out once they've hit a usable level, JLPT N3, then keep pushing for N2 once they're on the ground. Higher is always better. No real way around putting in the work long-term.

What if I have zero tech background at all?

That's most people who book. Path B exists because I started the same way — no CS degree, no connections. The first call is about mapping what you already have (even service/teaching experience) onto a realistic entry point, not assuming you already know networking or code.

What if the call turns out to not be useful for me?

Then I'll tell you that directly instead of trying to upsell you. The free 15-minute chat exists specifically so we find that out before you spend anything. If a paid session isn't the right next step for you, I'll say so.

How long does this actually take to work?

Depends on the path and how much time you put in weekly — there's no overnight version of this. Some routes (like JET) run on fixed yearly cycles no amount of momentum can compress; others move faster. The Transition Sprint is built around getting real forward motion inside 90 days — a plan, applications out, momentum — not a guarantee you'll be relocated by then. The free chat will give you an honest read on your specific timeline.

Is there an age limit for breaking into IT?

No fixed cutoff — the people I work with span roughly their late teens to their late 40s. The path can take more thought the further along your career you are, mainly because there's more to weigh and less runway to experiment. That's rarely a dealbreaker on its own, and it's exactly the kind of thing worth talking through. The free 15-minute chat is a low-risk way to get an honest read on what's realistic for you, whatever your age.
