Break into IT

No CS degree needed. I'll help you build a roadmap the way I built mine — certs and projects sized to the hours you actually have, not some bootcamp fantasy.

  • Which certs matter first, and which are a waste of money right now

Path C

Get an introduction

Some of this really is just who I know — my own company, old colleagues, other people who made the jump. It's not limited to IT or teaching — if it's a real fit for a role in Japan, tech or not, I'll introduce you. I'll be straight with you though: that's an introduction, not a promise. It depends on timing, fit, and whether there's actually an opening.

  • An introduction to a hiring manager or team, when it makes sense — inside IT or out
  • An honest read on whether you're ready for that intro yet
  • Job-hunt strategy for roles outside IT and teaching too, where I actually know the space
  • No promises — I can open a door, not walk you through it
Path D

Come on a working holiday visa

Japan has working holiday agreements with 30+ countries — a year in-country with the right to work, no employer or sponsor needed. It's not for everyone: age caps around 18–30 (18–25 for a handful of countries unless extended), usually one shot per lifetime, and it isn't open to every passport — Americans, notably, don't get this option at all. But if yours qualifies, it's one of the fastest, lowest-friction ways to actually get boots on the ground.

  • Whether your passport actually qualifies, and the real age cutoff for your country
  • How to use the year to build toward something instead of just traveling it away
  • Turning the working holiday into an actual work visa before the year runs out
  • Being straight about the ceiling — it's temporary, it isn't a work visa, and the clock doesn't stop
Path E

Study abroad on a language visa

A student visa gets you here long-term while you build the Japanese you actually need, not the classroom kind. Some people finance it and go full-time; others save up first. Either way, it buys you real time in-country and daily immersion no app or class back home comes close to.

  • Comparing schools that actually build your Japanese, not just your certificate wall
  • Realistic costs, and how people actually fund this
  • Using the visa as a soft landing — starting the job search while you're already here
  • What it doesn't do: it's not a work visa, part-time hours are capped, and you need a next step lined up before it runs out
Path F

Transfer with your current company

If your company already has an office here, or is looking to expand into Japan, an internal transfer is the safest path there is — you keep your employer and your track record instead of starting a cold job search from scratch.

  • How to actually check if your company has a Japan presence — most people never ask
  • How to raise it internally without sounding like you're already halfway out the door
  • What to expect if it moves forward — relocation support, timeline, visa handled through the company
  • An honest read on whether this door is even open for you — it isn't for everyone

Questions people actually ask

Skipping the FAQ fluff — these are the real doubts people email me with before booking.

How much Japanese will I need?

More than none — no sugar coating. Some people have the finances to move out and go straight into full-time language school. Most don't: they study alongside their current job and come out once they've hit a usable level, JLPT N3, then keep pushing for N2 once they're on the ground. Higher is always better. No real way around putting in the work long-term.

What if I have zero tech background at all?

That's most people who book. Path B exists because I started the same way — no CS degree, no connections. The first call is about mapping what you already have (even service/teaching experience) onto a realistic entry point, not assuming you already know networking or code.

What if the call turns out to not be useful for me?

Then I'll tell you that directly instead of trying to upsell you. The free 15-minute chat exists specifically so we find that out before you spend anything. If a paid session isn't the right next step for you, I'll say so.

How long does this actually take to work?

Depends on the path and how much time you put in weekly — there's no overnight version of this. Some routes (like JET) run on fixed yearly cycles no amount of momentum can compress; others move faster. The Transition Sprint is built around getting real forward motion inside 90 days — a plan, applications out, momentum — not a guarantee you'll be relocated by then. The free chat will give you an honest read on your specific timeline.

What happens right after I pay?

You'll get an email from me within 24 hours to lock in a time — I'm one person doing this alongside the day job and the podcast, so it's not instant, but it's fast.
